CSF is a guidance **for organizations to better manage and reduce their cybersecurity risks**.
It was created by the [[National Institute of Standard and Technology]], intended as a **comprehensive strategy for risk management and mitigation**.
It's become a helpful resource for corporate businesses and government entities.
# A Five-Fold Framework
The NIST CSF is broken down in five main areas:
- **Identify** the assets and processes that require security.
  This involves not only discovering assets at first, but also putting into place automated listing of all available assets, to know what you need to protect.
- **Protect** them with the right security measures.
  Deploying safeguards to protect your assets once you know about them is the first step towards security.
- **Detect** cybersecurity events relating to those assets.
  Monitoring and logging is extremely important to know how to respond to cybersecurity events.
  This also helps in prioritizing and quantifying vulnerabilities and risks.
- **Respond** to those events by mitigating their effects.
  This is about setting responsibility for each parts of a system to different persons or teams. Reporting at this stage is very important, to keep a big-picture view of what's happening in the organization.
  Of course, some risks are impossible to mitigate or eliminate, and must be accepted.
- **Recover** services that might have been impacted.
  The post-event phase is not any less critical than the others. It also involves making sure we're prepared if such an event were to happen again.
